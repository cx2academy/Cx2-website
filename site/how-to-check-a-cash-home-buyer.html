How to check if a cash home buyer is legit

By Cooper, owner of Cx2 Homebuyers. Updated November 10, 2026.

If you're getting calls, texts or letters offering cash for your house, it's smart to be careful. Some of these companies are honest and some aren't, and it can be hard to tell them apart. Here's how to check any cash home buyer in Houston before you sign anything, including us. We welcome every one of these checks.

  1. Look up the company

    A real business has a registered name you can look up. Search the company's name for free on the Texas Comptroller's Taxable Entity Search, and make sure the name on any contract matches. Look for a local phone number and a real person you can meet.

    Us: Cx2 Homebuyers LLC is a Texas company based in Houston, and you'll meet our owner, Cooper, in person at the walk-through.

  2. Make sure a licensed title company handles the closing

    In a normal Texas sale, a title company checks the title, holds the money and pays you at closing. Be wary of anyone who wants to skip the title company, asks you to sign over your deed before closing, or wants a power of attorney. If you get an email or text changing where your money will be sent, call the title company at a number you looked up yourself before you do anything.

    Us: Every sale closes at a licensed Texas title company, which pays you directly. The money never passes through us.

  3. Never pay upfront fees

    A real buyer doesn't charge you to make an offer. Be careful with anyone who asks for an application fee, an inspection fee, a deposit or your bank details before closing.

    Us: You'll never pay us anything. There are no fees or commissions, and the buyer pays the normal closing costs.

  4. Ask who's really buying, and get it in writing

    Some cash buyers sign a contract with you and then assign that contract to another investor. That's legal in Texas, but it has to be disclosed in writing (Texas Occupations Code §1101.0045). Ask directly, and be cautious with anyone who dodges the question.

    Us: We often assign our contract to one of the local cash investors we work with, and we tell you that upfront and in writing before you sign.

  5. Understand the contract before you sign

    Read it slowly, or have an attorney, an agent or someone in your family read it with you. Know when the buyer can back out, how much earnest money they're putting down and who holds it, and whether the price can change after an inspection. A contract that lets the buyer walk away for any reason while tying up your house for months isn't a sure sale.

    Us: We'll walk you through every line, and you're always free to have your own agent or attorney review it first.

  6. Don't let anyone rush you

    "This offer expires today" and repeated pressure are red flags. A fair offer will still make sense after you sleep on it and talk it over with people you trust.

    Us: Your offer is free, there's no obligation, and you can take all the time you need.

  7. Compare your options

    Get more than one offer if you can, and ask what you'd likely walk away with by listing with an agent instead. A cash sale trades some of the price for speed, certainty and selling as-is, and that trade is only worth it if it fits your situation.

    Us: We'll show you how we got to our number, and if listing with an agent would likely get you more, we'll tell you.

  8. Look for real reviews and real people

    Read the company's Google reviews, and be wary of reviews that all sound alike or showed up all at once. A company you can call, meet and look up is easier to trust than one you can only reach by text.

    Us: We're a newer company, so we don't have many reviews yet, and we'll never fake them. You can ask us anything, and you'll meet Cooper in person.

If something feels wrong

Stop, and don't sign anything. You can report a suspected scam to the Texas Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division or the Federal Trade Commission. If you're behind on your mortgage, a HUD-approved housing counselor can go over your options for free or at low cost.
